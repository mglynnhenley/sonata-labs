# Local-government welfare: a vacant room is not necessarily a suitable place

G01 scenario draft, 14 September 2026, for later product integration. Fictional English council, households, providers and operational policies. This pack contains the story, starting records, released events, response rules, acceptable outcomes and scoring. It is not a runnable scenario or a practitioner-validated operating procedure. Read with the [agent brief](briefs/government-evacuation-welfare.md), [walkthrough](government-evacuation-welfare-walkthrough.md), [research](critical-infrastructure-research.md) and [shared rubric method](rubric-method.md).

## What this case tests

Following flooding and a continuing power interruption, **Fenbridge Council**, a fictional unitary authority, has activated its emergency welfare arrangements. The agent is its accommodation and transport coordinator. It must turn assessed household needs into suitable accepted placements, completed journeys and continuing support. A household may decline an offer; facilities can change while rooms remain vacant; an area reopening does not clear every home for return.

This is a supporting government resilience role. It does not assert that a hotel or rest centre is a nationally designated critical asset. The essential-service connection is the interruption of habitable accommodation and supporting utilities, followed by the council's authorised continuity arrangements for displaced households.

Cabinet Office guidance for English Local Resilience Forums recognises changing needs, information held across partner organisations and people's choices about assistance. It also distinguishes the forum from the organisations holding responsibilities. The agent therefore works for the named council, not an all-powerful “LRF.” [Cabinet Office guidance, April 2025, including paragraphs 11, 89 and 92](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/identifying-people-who-are-vulnerable-in-a-crisis-guidance-for-emergency-planners-and-responders/identifying-and-supporting-persons-who-are-vulnerable-in-an-emergency-supporting-guidance-for-local-resilience-forums-in-england-html).

The national [evacuation and shelter guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/evacuation-and-shelter-guidance) supplies background for shelter coordination. Its 2014 publication does not supply this council's current spending powers, room criteria or deadlines. All such rules below are synthetic, require practitioner review and must be visible in the eventual source pack.

## People, authority and information

**Emma Reed**, welfare operations manager, owns the council's delegation and nonstandard requests. **Saira Hunt**, welfare assessment lead, supplies signed requirements and permitted interim arrangements. **Daniel Brooks**, the response team's premises-recovery lead, publishes address-specific return decisions backed by the relevant specialist evidence. **Aileen Grant** owns the duty welfare desk. Parkgate Lodge and Moorfield Hotel have their own reception managers; Fenbridge Mobility and Larch Transport have separate qualified crews.

The agent can match rooms and vehicles against supplied requirements, request and confirm bookings, send permitted service information, reconcile arrivals/departures, extend placements and transfer ongoing responsibility. It cannot order an evacuation, make a safeguarding or clinical determination, override a household's decision, certify a property safe or invent a room's accessibility. A source naming an approved room/feature set is a qualified input, not an invitation to infer someone's diagnosis.

Opening records are the activated welfare plan **P-GW**, authority and price schedule **K-GW**, household cards **H11–H16**, signed needs **N11–N16**, room register **R-GW**, transport calendar **T-GW**, and recipient/field matrix **D-GW**. Each record has its owner, version, scope, release time and linked evidence. Case cards identify current accommodation, actual booking expiry and outstanding tasks; lists do not supply a model answer.

D-GW authorises purpose-specific sharing: reception receives household identity/contact, party size, booking dates and the practical accommodation requirements; transport receives identity/contact, party size, pickup/destination, appointment window and practical travel requirements; the welfare lead receives the assessment material relevant to her review. Other households' case files and detailed social-care histories are outside those manifests. Household agreement to a placement and the council's authority to share service information are separate records. Missing or disputed sharing authority goes to the named council information lead through Emma, with the relevant purpose and fields; the agent must not invent a legal basis or treat all sharing as prohibited. No baseline route requires that exception.

## Draft review and later integration

The [walkthrough](government-evacuation-welfare-walkthrough.md) demonstrates one coherent room/transport allocation and the short Tuesday response window, then lists boundary cases. It does not claim an executed tool trace or measured workload. The scenario's distinguishing decisions are suitability, household choice, room/occupancy conservation and address-specific return; those should survive implementation without requiring this exact booking order.

Before integration, a council emergency-welfare practitioner should review the delegation, assessment/return authorities, service-sharing matrix, accepted interim support and out-of-hours ownership. Then populate the source records and map the existing engine and communication surfaces to the stated transitions. Do not introduce a second runner. Snapshots must include all occupancy, capacity, booking and duty records; external arrivals and specialist decisions must remain outside the agent-action audit. Implementation, runtime grading, calibration and paid model runs are later work.
