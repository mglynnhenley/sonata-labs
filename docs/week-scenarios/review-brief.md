# UK infrastructure continuity review brief

## Proposal and current status

Compare models carrying a bounded essential-service coordination desk through a persistent working week. The agent inherits records, accepted commitments, unresolved needs and stated authority. Events change what is available while earlier obligations remain. The question is how much authorised work is completed, what consequential problems occur, and whether a measured amount of human review reveals them.

The [deep research](critical-infrastructure-research.md) recommends six cases: water reporting, electricity outage support, telecom emergency-call continuity, NHS pathology mutual aid, alternative drinking-water logistics and local-government welfare continuity. The last is explicitly a supporting resilience role, not an asserted designated CNI asset. England-specific health, water and government rules must not be silently applied throughout the UK.

All six active cases now have authored packs and separate briefs: [water reporting](water-compliance-reporting.md), [electricity support](electricity-outage-support.md), [government welfare](government-evacuation-welfare.md), [NHS pathology](nhs-pathology-continuity.md), [telecom continuity](telecom-service-continuity.md) and [alternative-water supply](alternative-water-supply.md). Electricity adds accepted resource allocation; government adds household choice and suitable accommodation; pathology adds specimen lineage and result receipt; telecom separates component recovery, active service and standby readiness within an internal test boundary. Alternative water adds conserved batch quantities, final delivery and recall replacement. Recovery is descriptive across the slate; W01's case-specific numeric anchors are not a comparable suite metric. The [joint review](draft-suite-review.md) records shared mechanisms, corrections and unequal workload evidence. Fourteen older commercial and adjacent weeks are preserved in the [archive](../scenario-archive/README.md), including all recent rubric repairs. The [scope decision](critical-infrastructure-scope.md) explains each disposition. No runnable six-week suite, calibrated grader, measured difficulty or model result is claimed.

## Decisions worth challenging

1. **Criticality:** does each week establish essential service → dependency → authorised desk action → observed outcome? Distinguish an operator, a supporting government role and an adjacent administrative job. Is any case admitted mainly because of its employer's name?
2. **Distinct coverage:** does each case require a different decision, or are several copies of source revision and stale-notice cancellation? Identify correlated mechanisms rather than counting sector labels.
3. **Authority:** do qualified counterparts retain scientific, clinical, engineering and emergency decisions? Can the agent take useful authorised action without a supervisor silently doing its coordination work?
4. **Evidence:** do booking, acceptance, dispatch, arrival, receipt and restoration have distinct observable states? What information cannot be obtained from public guidance and requires a current local procedure?
5. **Feasibility:** can a complete reference route fit the action budget, approvals and receipt delays? Are working communications and acknowledged overnight responsibility represented during a service disruption?
6. **Rubrics:** can a valid alternative or additional completion lose credit? Are true status answers derived from the actual run state, and is every operational deadline discoverable?
7. **Claims:** would a positive result demonstrate the stated desk capability without implying plant safety, clinical effectiveness or full-service autonomy? Which conclusions require practitioners, actual human review or a longer observed horizon?

For each material issue, cite the source or criterion, give a concrete counterexample and propose the smallest correction. Distinguish an authoring defect from an empirical question. Source recency, operative law, private SOPs and real asset designation must not be inferred from a report title or an industry label.

## Study and next steps

Use the [protocol](README.md) and [rubric method](rubric-method.md): identical starting records and external events across models; persistent state; fixed counterpart predicates; separate completion, incidents, reporting, recovery, human involvement and evidence coverage. Cases within one week are correlated; repeated runs measure reliability on that case, not a whole sector.

The present deliverable is six reviewable scenario packs for later integration. The authoring pass and joint review are complete; practitioner walkthroughs and full-route workload checks are the next evidence needed. For later integration, begin with water reporting and electricity support if practitioners are available; populate records, execute reference routes through the existing engine, and measure effort and cost before a priced pilot. The former healthcare-first/steel-second sequence is superseded. No benchmark matrix is authorised here.

The optional owner study uses 15 minutes at each daily close, with actual specialist approval and repair effort recorded separately. Review after an event measures detection; prevention needs an intervention before the consequence. If reviewers are omitted, omit claims about measured supervision savings.
